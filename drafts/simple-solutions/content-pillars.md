## 1. Follow-up gaps

**Purpose:** Show owners who already run ads where a lead goes quiet after the click or the call, and what a simple fix looks like, without claiming we measured their loss.

**Audience:** Local service businesses already running ads. The person who feels the leads "aren't picking up" or "came in and then nothing."

**Formats:** Facebook carousel or single card on the existing Page, Instagram carousel or single card on the existing account, LinkedIn post once the company Page exists (it does not yet).

**CTA:** Book a free Zoom call: message us or email cnavarro.business@gmail.com for the link.

**Post ideas**

1. **The lead came in. Then what?** Carousel, FB + IG (LI when the Page exists). Five frames: ad click, form or call, who was supposed to answer, what "no answer" looks like, one next step they can check this week. No stat about how fast a lead dies.
2. **Missed call after the ad.** Single card + caption, all three platforms. The ad did its job; the phone went to voicemail. Explain missed-call text-back as a process (a text goes out when the call is missed). Do not promise a booking from it.
3. **The form that lands in a mailbox nobody opens.** Carousel. Ask where form leads go today. Educational. No client story.
4. **Ads and follow-up are two jobs.** LI text post, with a shorter FB/IG card. One person can own the ad and a different step can own the reply. They should not be blurred. This matches how the work is separated. It is not a result.
5. **A quiet pipeline.** Carousel. What it looks like when a lead has no stage (new, contacted, booked, closed, or similar plain words). Invite them to look at whether every lead has a stage. No "we recovered X leads."

## 2. The page the ad already sends people to

**Purpose:** Help owners check the page, listing, and phone number their current ads point at. Clarity tips only. No before/after client results. The attached plan marks real before/after as missing proof. Keep it that way.

**Audience:** Local service businesses already running ads, especially when the ad is on but the calls do not match what they expected. Do not tell them they need a new website as the default.

**Formats:** FB carousel, IG carousel, LI post. One news-style post may be a link share on FB and LI and a headline card on IG, only after the source is checked again and Cesar approves. Facebook's organic link cap is described in the week-1 draft. **OPEN** whether this Page is affected. Do not add a link until that is confirmed at publish time.

**CTA:** Book a free Zoom call: message us or email cnavarro.business@gmail.com for the link.

**Post ideas**

1. **Four checks on the page your ad uses.** Carousel. Headline, phone number, the offer in one sentence, and whether it is readable on a phone. Generic example only ("a local service company"), never a real client.
2. **A new website will not fix an ads problem.** Carousel, from the week-1 draft's third post. And ads will not fix a page that hides the phone number. Four questions: what should a customer do, how do people find you now, what is broken in your words, who approves the next step. No performance claim. The week-1 draft's "who approves spend" question is fine as a process question. Do not attach a budget figure.
3. **The number on the ad, the page, and the listing.** Single card. They should match. No claim that a mismatch caused a measured loss.
4. **Someone can suggest an edit to your Google listing.** News repost plus our take, only with the source named. The week-1 draft records a Search Engine Roundtable article (Barry Schwartz, Sep 23, 2026) and Google's help page. **OPEN** for this drafts file: re-open that source before publish. Do not post the four-day claim from memory. Our take stays: check which email gets listing alerts, and keep hours and phone on the website matching the listing.
5. **What the ad promised vs what the page says.** Carousel. Read the ad's first line and the page's first line side by side. If they describe different offers, fix the words before asking the ad to do more. No ROAS, no cost language.

## 3. Meta ads, explained as a process

**Purpose:** Teach the review, not the trophy. Objective, where the lead goes (form or page), and what a person checks before changing a campaign. No cost-per-lead figures, no return claims, no "we scaled" stories.

**Audience:** Local service businesses already running Meta ads, who sit in a monthly check-in and are not sure which number matters.

**Formats:** FB carousel, IG carousel, LI text or document-style post. Screen mocks, if any, use a fake business ("Local Service Co." in the week-1 creative notes) and a visible sample-data label. Do not show a real Ads Manager account.

**CTA:** Book a free Zoom call: message us or email cnavarro.business@gmail.com for the link.

**Post ideas**

1. **Pick the objective before you touch the campaign.** Carousel. Leads, calls, or messages are different jobs. Say which one the account is actually using. Process only.
2. **Form vs landing page.** Single card + caption. When the ad opens a form and when it opens a page. No claim that one is cheaper or better.
3. **What a check-in is for.** LI post, short IG/FB card. Hook, whether people click, and whether the leads match the work they do. Definitions only. The exact list of checks Cesar uses in a real account is **OPEN** (week-1 draft, decision C12). Do not invent "the six things I check."
4. **Creative and offer, before more reach.** Carousel. Look at whether the offer is clear and the first line is specific. Do not say a past headline hit a click rate. Do not mention a client's views or a cost per result.
5. **Retargeting in plain language.** Carousel. People who already visited or engaged, and a message aimed at them. No library of a real client's scripts, no performance number. The résumé's retargeting-script work stays off the post unless a later, sourced draft says otherwise. **OPEN** for public use.

## 5. After the click: the lead system

**Purpose:** Explain the offer behind the ads: an instant reply, a missed-call text-back, and a pipeline so a lead has a place to go. Process and definitions. This is the fifth pillar, added so the core offer is teachable without a price.

**Audience:** Local service businesses already running ads, who have leads and do not have a written path for what happens next.

**Formats:** FB carousel, IG carousel, LI post. Same sample-data rule. No screenshot of a live GoHighLevel account.

**CTA:** Book a free Zoom call: message us or email cnavarro.business@gmail.com for the link.

**Post ideas**

1. **Instant reply, defined.** Carousel. A message the business prepares in advance, sent when a new lead arrives, so the lead is not staring at silence. Do not claim a response-time record.
2. **Missed-call text-back, defined.** Single card. The call is missed; a text goes out with a way to reconnect. Process only. No deliverability percentage. The A2P approval figure is a team result from another role and is **not** a social proof point. **OPEN** even for a vague "we handle texting compliance" line. The week-1 draft still treats public CRM / A2P positioning as a Cesar decision. Until that is a yes, this post stays educational and does not say we register anything.
3. **One pipeline.** Carousel. Every lead has a stage and an owner. Ads do not replace that list.
4. **What we will not promise.** LI post plus a short FB/IG card. We will not promise a cost, a lead count, a ranking, or a full calendar. We will look at the page, the follow-up path, and the ads they already run.
5. **A walkthrough, not a dashboard tour.** Carousel. Three boxes: reply, missed call, pipeline stage. Invite the Zoom call. If a future portfolio prototype illustrates this, use it only under the rules in `pillar-to-portfolio-map.md`. The prototype does not exist in the site repo yet.
