Concept · October 2026

When lead costs spike, show the evidence before anyone touches the budget

When lead costs spike, owners panic and agencies guess. I designed a copilot that shows its evidence, separates diagnosis from action, and never moves budget without approval.

Cesar Navarro · cnavarro.business@gmail.com

Role
Concept direction
Tools
AI-assisted build (Claude, Cursor)
Problem
A week of expensive leads makes a shop owner panic and pushes an account manager to change the budget on a hunch. The ads manager, the CRM, and the landing page each tell a different story, and none of them say what was not checked.
User
Camila, an account manager at the fictional agency Mesa Local, and Mateo, who owns the fictional shop Solano Plumbing and has to approve any budget move.
Decisions
  1. One campaign, one sentence. The alert names the spike. The other campaigns stay quiet so the page does not treat a normal week as an emergency.
  2. Diagnosis is not a button that spends money. Checks are ranked. Each one links to the demo rows it used. Confidence is a word, not a percent. A list says what the file did not contain.
  3. Proposed changes are previewed and separate. Pause the tired creative, move budget only inside the owner's cap, or request new creative. A person approves each one. Undo puts it back. The budget does not move until the owner approves.
  4. The note to the owner is five lines. English or Spanish, editable, and copy and export work. The page does not send it.
Outcome
A working prototype of the alert, the checks, the guarded changes, and the note, with empty, loading, file-error, and approve-error states. Not tested with account managers or owners. No real ad account was connected.
Label
Concept. The agency, the shop, the people, and the numbers are fictional. No 3D. The chart is flat on purpose.
